Tokenization-Aware Protein Language Modeling

A controlled study of evolution-guided protein units, vocabulary size, fine-tuning strategy, and dynamic biological knowledge fusion.

Amirreza Sattarzadeh · Burak Suyunu · Özdeniz Dolu · Arzucan Özgür

LifeLU · TABILAB · Department of Computer Engineering, Boğaziçi University

The central question

When do biologically informed units earn their added complexity?

Protein language models are usually fed single amino acids or statistically learned subwords. We test whether evolution-guided units and sequence-derived dynamic knowledge improve downstream performance—and whether any gain survives careful controls for vocabulary size, training strategy, data, and compute.

Study design

Separate the effect of the tokenizer from the choices around it.

All tokenizer families are evaluated within a consistent ESM-2 35M framework pretrained on UniRef50, then transferred across the PETA task suite under multiple fine-tuning regimes.

Amino acids
The standard residue-level baseline: compact, universal, and maximally granular.
BPE
Frequency-driven subword units learned from the sequence corpus.
PUMA families
Protein units discovered through mutation-aware merging guided by amino-acid substitution matrices.
Dynamics fusion
A parallel arm that injects pooled sequence-derived dynamics features and measures their marginal value.

What the sweep shows

There is no context-free “best” protein tokenizer.

The practical choice depends on task granularity, data, vocabulary size, fine-tuning strategy, and available compute.

Main takeaway

Choose the full configuration, not the tokenizer name.

Biological priors can be useful, but they do not dominate every task. A fair decision compares accuracy and robustness together with sequence length, vocabulary cost, trainable parameters, and inference budget.

Vocabulary size is a first-class variable.

Performance often changes non-monotonically with vocabulary scale; larger is not automatically better, and very large vocabularies can sharply reduce effective model capacity.

Task and pooling choices interact.

Per-protein and per-residue tasks respond differently. Attention-based pooling is frequently stronger than simple mean pooling, but the gain remains configuration-dependent.
